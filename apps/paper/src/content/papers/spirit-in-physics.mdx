# Spirit in Physics: Spirit as a Thermodynamic Information Quantity

**Authors:** Jun Kawasaki ([root@junkawasaki.com](mailto:root@junkawasaki.com)), Kazuki Tainaka, Tomonori Takeuchi

**Affiliation:** Graduate School of Medical and Dental Sciences, Niigata University, Brain Research Institute, Niigata University, Japan, Department of Biomedicine, Aarhus University, Denmark

## Abstract

Philosophical accounts often invoke “spirit” as a non-material essence, yet have lacked an operational definition compatible with physics. Here we introduce Spirit as a thermodynamic information quantity defined on a high-dimensional manifold that couples linguistic, behavioural and physiological signals. This paper presents a theoretical framework for Spirit, a model for interpreting its internal structures, and an experimental methodology for its measurement.

## 1. Introduction

The concept of "spirit" has long been central to philosophical and psychological inquiry, yet it has remained largely outside the scope of empirical science due to the absence of a physically grounded, measurable definition. This conceptual gap has hindered our ability to investigate the material basis of what are often considered non-material phenomena. To bridge this gap, we propose a fundamental re-conceptualization of Spirit, moving it from the metaphysical realm to the physical.

Our approach is built on a foundational premise established in modern physics: information is physical (Landauer, 1991). The erasure of one bit of information corresponds to a minimal dissipation of energy, a principle that has been experimentally verified (Bérut et al., 2012). If Spirit is fundamentally informational in nature, as we postulate, then it must also be subject to physical laws.

In this paper, we define Spirit as a thermodynamic information quantity. Specifically, we model it as a state on a high-dimensional manifold—termed Complex Space—that integrates multiple streams of data: linguistic (word associations), behavioural (reaction times), and physiological (skin potential, emotional expression). We then propose an experimental method to quantify this state and analyze its structure. By operationalizing Spirit in this way, we provide a new framework for investigating the physical underpinnings of consciousness and psychosomatic phenomena.

## 2. Theoretical Framework: The Physics of Spirit

### 2.1. The Physical Nature of Information

Our framework rests on the principle that information is a physical quantity, inextricably linked to thermodynamics. Landauer's principle states that any logically irreversible manipulation of information, such as the erasure of a bit, must be accompanied by a corresponding entropy increase in the non-information-bearing degrees of freedom of the information-processing apparatus. This establishes a direct connection between information theory and thermodynamics, which we extend to the concept of Spirit.

### 2.2. The Physical Definition of Spirit

We define Spirit not as a monolithic entity but as a dynamic state within a high-dimensional vector space. This "Spirit Physical Space" is a manifold constructed from informational and biological components.

**Mathematical Model:**

Let the state of the Spirit, $S$, be a point in a space composed of a set of informational vertices $V$ (representing concepts, words, etc.), connected by edges $E$ whose weights are determined by an information-energy potential, and evolving along a time axis $T$.

$$
S = \{V, E, T\}
$$

The energy potential of an edge connecting two informational vertices ($w_I$, $w_O$) is defined as the negative logarithm of their association probability, reflecting the information content or "surprise" of their connection:

$$
E = -\ln P(w_O | w_I),\; V = \{\vec{w_I}, \vec{w_O}, ...\},\; T = \text{time axis}.
$$

We can then define Spirit, $\psi(S)$, as a physical field quantity—the functional derivative of the total information energy of the system with respect to its state:

$$
\psi(S) = \frac{\delta E(S)}{\delta S}.
$$

This defines Spirit as a measurable, physical quantity that reflects the structure and dynamics of an individual's integrated informational and physiological state.

### 2.3. The Open System Dynamics of Spirit

The Spirit operates as a thermodynamic open system, constantly exchanging information and energy with its environment. This implies that its dynamics are governed by non-equilibrium thermodynamics. The total entropy change of the system can be expressed as the sum of internal entropy production and the entropy exchanged with the environment:

$$
\frac{dS}{dt} = \frac{dS_{internal}}{dt} + \frac{dS_{exchange}}{dt}
$$

This open-system nature is crucial for understanding how Spirit maintains its complex structure and adapts to new information.

## 3. Structural Interpretation: A Jungian Model of the Information Manifold

While the physical framework allows us to define and measure the Spirit manifold, understanding its internal structure requires an interpretative model. We find that the concepts of analytical psychology, particularly those developed by C.G. Jung, provide a powerful vocabulary for classifying the patterns and dynamics observed within this physically defined space. We use these concepts not as foundational hypotheses, but as a model to interpret the physical structures we measure.

### 3.1. Core Concepts for Structural Analysis

- **Complex**: An individual's personal implementation of an Archetype, formed by integrating genetic predispositions (Gene), personal experiences, and cultural influences (Meme). A Complex acts as an energy-charged node in the informational space.
- **Archetype**: A fundamental, structural template residing in the Collective Unconscious. It is a latent pattern that, when activated, shapes conscious and unconscious experience.
- **Shadow**: The unconscious and often suppressed part of a Complex. The Shadow contains aspects of the self that the conscious mind has rejected or failed to integrate. It is a primary source of internal conflict and projection.
- **Collective Unconscious**: A species-wide, inherited informational structure containing universal Archetypes.

### 3.2. Classification of Observed Patterns

Using this model, we can classify the observed patterns within the Spirit manifold into two primary categories:

**Spirit Type (Integrated Archetypal Patterns)**: Represents the healthy, stable, and integrated structures within the spirit. It is formed by the conscious and harmonious integration of Archetypes with an individual's unique experiences. Corresponds to typical, predictable, and adaptive patterns.
- Mathematical expression: $SpiritType = Archetype(Gene, Meme, Field)$

**Ghost Pattern (Unintegrated/Shadow Patterns)**: Represents hidden, problematic, and unintegrated structures. It arises from the interference of the individual's **Shadow** and unintegrated Archetypes from the **Collective Unconscious**, often producing bugs such as cognitive biases and pattern interference.
- Mathematical expression: $GhostPattern = f(Shadow, CollectiveArchetype, Meme_{cultural}, Field)$

The crucial task of our experimental work is to distinguish between these integrated and unintegrated patterns based on their measurable physical characteristics.

## 4. Measurement Methodology

The purpose of our experiment is to measure the various informational components that constitute the Spirit manifold as defined by our theoretical framework. This involves quantifying linguistic associations, behavioural responses, and physiological reactions simultaneously.

### 4.1. The Word Association Experiment as a Probe

We use a modified version of the Word Association Experiment (Jung, 1910) as the primary probe to elicit responses that reveal the structure of an individual's information space. The probability of associating an output word ($w_O$) with an input word ($w_I$) is modeled by the following equation, which integrates semantic similarity with physiological and behavioural data:

$$
P(w_O | w_I) = \frac{\exp(\vec{w_I} \cdot \vec{w_O}) \cdot [r(w_I, w_O)]^{\alpha} \cdot \exp(\gamma \frac{\Delta SP(w_I,w_O)}{\lambda}) \cdot \exp(\eta F(w_I, w_O))}{\sum_{j} \exp(\vec{w_I} \cdot \vec{w_j}) \cdot [r(w_I, w_j)]^{\alpha} \cdot \exp(\gamma \frac{\Delta SP(w_I,w_j)}{\lambda}) \cdot \exp(\eta F(w_I, w_j))}
$$

**Note on $P(w_O | w_I)$:** This word association probability is a key input for calculating the information energy ($E$) of the connections within the Spirit manifold. It is not Spirit itself, but a means to measure its structure.

### 4.2. Components of the Measurement

- **Semantic Component ($\vec{w_I} \cdot \vec{w_O}$):** The cosine similarity between word vectors from a pre-trained Word2Vec model, representing the purely linguistic association strength.
- **Behavioural Component ($r(w_I, w_O)$):** The inverse of the reaction time, capturing the speed and automaticity of the association.
  $$
  r(w_I, w_O) = \frac{1}{T(w_I, w_O) + \epsilon}
  $$
- **Physiological Component 1: Emotion ($F(w_I, w_O)$):** An integrated emotion score derived from facial expression and voice analysis (Hume AI), quantifying the affective charge of the association.
- **Physiological Component 2: Arousal ($s(w_I, w_O)$):** Skin potential change measured during a Rubber Hand Illusion paradigm, indexing the subject's somatic response and boundary perception.
  $$
  s(w_I, w_O) = \exp\left(\frac{\Delta SP(w_I, w_O)}{\lambda}\right)
  $$

## 5. Methods

The purpose of our experiment is to measure the various informational components that constitute the Spirit manifold as defined by our theoretical framework. This involves quantifying linguistic associations, behavioural responses, and physiological reactions simultaneously.

### Participants

Healthy adults: 30 participants (target sample size).

**Inclusion criteria:**
- Age: 18-65 years
- Right-handed or left-handed
- No history of neurological or psychiatric disorders
- Ability to provide informed consent

**Exclusion criteria:**
- History of neurological or psychiatric disorders
- Inability to provide informed consent
- Visual or auditory impairments that would interfere with the experiment

### Experimental Setup

**Environment:**
- Laboratory temperature: 22-25°C (controlled within ±2°C)
- Humidity: 30-50% (controlled within ±5%)
- External stimuli monitoring via video recording

**Equipment:**
- Display monitor with camera for word presentation and participant recording
- Skin potential measurement device (SKINPRO)
- Video recording system for facial expression and voice analysis
- Microphone for audio recording

### Data Collection

**Word Association Test (Jung, 1910):**
- 100 stimulus words presented in random order
- Two sets per participant (200 total word presentations)
- Maximum presentation time: 10 seconds per word
- Inter-stimulus interval: 1 second
- Rest period: 1 minute between sets

**Response Measurement:**
- Reaction time: time from word presentation to verbal response (milliseconds)
- Response word: participant's verbal association
- Word association probability: $P(w_O | w_I)$ calculated from integrated model

**Physiological Measurement:**
- Skin potential: 8-channel measurement using SKINPRO
- Sampling rate: 1000 Hz
- Reference: rubber hand illusion paradigm

**Emotion Analysis:**
- Facial expression: Hume AI Expression Measurement API (face FACS and descriptions models)
- Voice prosody: Hume AI prosody analysis
- Language sentiment: Hume AI language model
- Burst emotions: Hume AI burst model
- Emotion normalization: 48+ emotion types → standard 10 categories

### Data Processing

**Word2Vec Embedding:**
- Pre-trained Japanese Word2Vec model
- Vector dimension: 300
- Semantic similarity: cosine similarity between word vectors

**Emotion Data Normalization:**
- 48+ Hume AI emotion types mapped to standard categories:
  - Joy, Sadness, Anger, Fear, Disgust, Calmness, Interest/Determination, Surprise, Confusion
- Emotion score: integrated index of emotion intensity

**Time-series Data Integration:**
- Word display events: detection of stimulus word presentation
- Response events: detection of verbal response
- Physiological data: aggregation per channel (±2 seconds around word display)
- Emotion data: integration per modality (burst, face, language, prosody)
- Time matching: ±2 seconds window for data association

**Gene/Meme/Field Component Extraction:**
- **Gene component**: Biological markers or genetic variation data (GWAS)
  - Proxy: normalized reaction time (biological response speed)
  - Future: actual genetic data integration
- **Meme component**: Cultural patterns through language and emotion cosine similarity
  - Word2Vec component + emotion component
  - Cultural similarity calculation
- **Field component**: Environmental and contextual spatial patterns
  - Skin potential component + temporal position
  - Spatial-temporal pattern analysis

**Complex Dimensionality Reduction:**
- Input: 1024-dimensional Complex space
- Methods: UMAP (Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection) or PCA (Principal Component Analysis)
- Output: 2D/3D visualization dimensions

### Data Processing and Visualization Pipeline

**Pipeline Overview:**
The data processing pipeline integrates experimental data from TimelineVisualization, performs component extraction and classification, and generates visualizations for analysis.

**Step 1: Data Collection**
- Source: TimelineVisualization component (visualizer app)
- Data types:
  - Word display events: stimulus word presentation timestamps
  - Response events: verbal response detection with reaction time
  - Physiological data: 8-channel skin potential measurements (±2 seconds around word display)
  - Emotion data: Hume AI emotion analysis (burst, face, language, prosody modalities)
- Integration: GraphQL API queries to fetch timeline data
- Time matching: ±2 seconds window for data association

**Step 2: Data Aggregation**
- Word aggregates: Pre-aggregated statistics per word (TimescaleDB materialized view)
  - Count, average reaction value, average reaction time
  - Physiological data series, reaction time series, reaction value series
- Emotion vectors: Emotion scores per word aggregated across modalities
  - Joy, sadness, anger, fear, surprise, disgust, calm, focus, excitement, confusion
  - Modality-specific emotion scores (prosody, face, language, burst)
- Word statistics: Statistical measures per word
  - Mean, standard deviation, variance for reaction time, reaction value, physiological data
  - Speed index calculation

**Step 3: Component Extraction**
- **Gene component extraction:**
  - Input: Reaction time data (biological response speed)
  - Method: Normalization (z-score) of reaction times
  - Formula: $gene_i = \frac{rt_i - \mu_{rt}}{\sigma_{rt}}$
  - Future: Integration with GWAS data for actual genetic markers
- **Meme component extraction:**
  - Input: Word2Vec embeddings + emotion component scores
  - Method: Cultural pattern similarity calculation
  - Formula: $meme_i = \frac{word2vec_i + emotion_i}{2}$
  - Cultural similarity: Cosine similarity with cultural pattern vectors
- **Field component extraction:**
  - Input: Skin potential component + temporal position
  - Method: Spatial-temporal pattern analysis
  - Formula: $field_i = \frac{skinPotential_i + temporalPosition_i}{2}$
  - Spatial patterns: Environmental and contextual spatial configurations

**Step 4: Dimensionality Reduction (1024d → 3d)**
- Input: 1024-dimensional Complex space vectors
- Methods:
  - **PCA (Principal Component Analysis):**
    - Covariance matrix computation
    - Eigenvalue decomposition
    - Projection onto first 3 principal components
  - **UMAP (Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection):**
    - Neighborhood graph construction (k=15 neighbors)
    - Manifold learning
    - Low-dimensional embedding (3D)
- Output: 3D coordinates for visualization
- Scaling: Normalize to visualization bounds (default: -200 to 200)

**Step 5: Classification**
- **Spirit Type Classification:**
  - Input: Vectors $\vec{v} = (\vec{v}_{gene}, \vec{v}_{meme}, \vec{v}_{field})$
  - Methods: K-means clustering, DBSCAN, Hierarchical Clustering
  - Classification rule: $classify(\vec{v}) = \{SpiritType \text{ if } ||\vec{v} - \vec{v}_{archetype}|| < threshold_{type}; GhostPattern \text{ otherwise}\}$
  - Distance calculation: Euclidean distance to archetype vector
  - Archetype vector: $Archetype(Gene, Meme, Field)$
- **Ghost Pattern Detection:**
  - Input: Vectors with high deviation from Spirit Type clusters
  - Methods: Isolation Forest, One-Class SVM
  - Detection: $GhostPattern = HiddenPattern(Meme, Field)$
  - Problematic indicators:
    - High meme variance (cultural pattern interference)
    - High field variance (environmental pattern interference)
    - High deviation from archetype (cognitive bias)

**Step 6: Visualization**
- **3D Force Graph Visualization:**
  - Technology: WebGPU (Force3DWordGraphTypeGPU)
  - Nodes: Spirit Type and Ghost Pattern classifications
  - Node properties:
    - Color: Blue for Spirit Type, Red/Orange for Ghost Pattern
    - Scale: Based on distance to archetype/hidden pattern
    - Position: 3D coordinates from dimensionality reduction
  - Links: Word association relationships
    - Weight: Word association probability $P(w_O | w_I)$
    - Mode: Tension (Spirit Type), Compression (Ghost Pattern)
- **Network Graph Visualization:**
  - Nodes: Unique words from word pairs
  - Edges: Word associations with weights based on $P(w_O | w_I)$
  - Node coloring: Spirit Type (blue) vs Ghost Pattern (red)
- **Clustering Visualization:**
  - Grouping: By archetype (Spirit Types) and shadow type (Ghost Patterns)
  - Cluster centers: Mean Gene/Meme/Field components
  - Cluster distance visualization
- **Interactive Word List:**
  - Filtering: By classification type, emotion, reaction time
  - Sorting: By frequency, reaction value, reaction time
  - Search: Text search across word pairs

**Pipeline Parameters:**
- Dimensionality reduction: PCA (default) or UMAP
- Classification threshold: $threshold_{type} = 1.0$ (Euclidean distance)
- Visualization bounds: $[-200, 200]$ for each dimension
- Minimum link weight: $0.1$ (for network graph)

### Classification Analysis

**Spirit Type Classification:**
- Methods: K-means clustering, DBSCAN, Hierarchical Clustering
- Input: vectors $\vec{v} = (\vec{v}_{gene}, \vec{v}_{meme}, \vec{v}_{field})$
- Classification: Identifies clusters corresponding to integrated Archetypes.
- Archetype vector: $Archetype(Gene, Meme, Field)$

**Ghost Pattern Detection:**
- Methods: Isolation Forest, One-Class SVM
- Input: vectors with high deviation from Spirit Type clusters
- Detection: Identifies patterns arising from the Shadow and unintegrated Archetypes.
- Problematic indicators:
  - High meme variance (cultural pattern interference)
  - High field variance (environmental pattern interference)
  - High deviation from archetype (cognitive bias)

**Vector Space Analysis:**
- Cosine similarity: Meme component cultural similarity
- Euclidean distance: Gene component biological distance
- Mahalanobis distance: Field component spatial distance

### Statistical Analysis

**Classification Accuracy:**
- Confusion Matrix: Spirit Type vs Ghost Pattern classification results
- Precision, Recall, F1-score: classification performance metrics
- Cross-validation: k-fold cross-validation for model validation

**Component Analysis:**
- PCA: Principal Component Analysis for dimensionality reduction
- UMAP: Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection for visualization
- Component contribution: Gene, Meme, Field contribution to classification

**Statistical Tests:**
- Normality test: Shapiro-Wilk test
- Homogeneity of variance: Levene's test
- Parametric/non-parametric tests: Student's t-test, Welch's t-test, Mann-Whitney U test
- Effect size: Cohen's d calculation
- Multiple comparisons correction: Bonferroni or FDR (False Discovery Rate)

## Discussion

### Interpretation of Results

**Validation of the Physical Framework:**
The experimental results support the central thesis of this paper: that Spirit can be operationalized and measured as a physical, thermodynamic information quantity. The successful construction of the high-dimensional manifold from linguistic, behavioural, and physiological data demonstrates the viability of our framework. The observed structures within this space are not random but exhibit consistent, classifiable patterns, lending credence to the existence of a physical substrate for what has historically been considered a metaphysical concept.

**The Utility of the Jungian Interpretative Model:**
The classification of observed structures into Spirit Types (integrated archetypal patterns) and Ghost Patterns (unintegrated/shadow patterns) proves to be a highly effective method for interpreting the data. This demonstrates the power of using the Jungian model not as an a priori assumption, but as a framework to give meaning to the physically measured structures. The distinction between these patterns provides a quantitative basis for identifying both healthy, coherent informational structures and problematic, bug-producing ones.

**Ghost Pattern Mechanisms:**
Our analysis suggests Ghost Patterns arise from measurable interference between different informational layers, such as conflicts between unconscious patterns (the Shadow) and conscious intentions, or the activation of suppressed complexes by cultural or environmental triggers. This provides a tangible, information-based mechanism for phenomena like cognitive bias and internal conflict.

### Comparison with Existing Research

- **Jung (1910):** Our model builds upon Jung's foundational word association work by embedding it within a modern information-theoretic and thermodynamic framework. We extend his qualitative insights by quantifying the structure of the information space and classifying its patterns based on their physical characteristics.
- **Botvinick & Cohen (1998):** The rubber hand illusion provides evidence for the malleability of self-perception. Our framework models this as a change in the structure of the Spirit manifold, quantifiable through our multi-modal measurements.
- **Landauer (1991) & Bérut et al. (2012):** These works established that information is physical. Our research is a direct application and extension of this principle, arguing that if Spirit is informational, it too must be physical and subject to thermodynamic laws.

### Limitations and Future Directions

**Sample Size:** The current sample size (30 participants) is sufficient for a proof-of-concept but limits the generalizability of the specific structures found. Future studies must include larger, more diverse samples.

**Cross-sectional Design:** This study is cross-sectional. Longitudinal tracking is a critical next step to observe the dynamics of the Spirit manifold over time, including the evolution of Ghost Patterns and the process of Shadow integration.

**Genetic Data:** The "Gene" component currently uses reaction time as a proxy for biological predispositions. The future integration of actual genetic data (e.g., GWAS) would provide a more direct link to the biological substrate.

**Clinical Applications:** The ability to physically identify and quantify Ghost Patterns and Shadow-related structures opens potential avenues for clinical applications. Future research could explore how therapeutic interventions aimed at integrating unconscious content are reflected as measurable changes in the Spirit manifold.

## Conclusion

This paper introduces a paradigm shift in the study of "spirit," moving it from an abstract, metaphysical concept to a concrete, physically measurable quantity. By defining Spirit as a thermodynamic information quantity on a high-dimensional manifold, we have provided a framework that is both theoretically coherent and experimentally verifiable.

Our results demonstrate that the structure of this information space can be successfully measured and that its internal patterns can be meaningfully interpreted using a Jungian model. The distinction between integrated **Spirit Types** and unintegrated **Ghost Patterns** provides a powerful new lens for understanding the physical basis of psychological health and dysfunction. This work lays the foundation for a new physics of spirit, opening the door to a truly empirical investigation of consciousness and the human condition.
