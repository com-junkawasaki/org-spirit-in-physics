# Candidate empirical study: target-specific self-inclusion

Status: **proposal for author and ethics review**, 2026-09-28. No new participants, analyses, results, preregistration or institutional approval are claimed here. This proposal implements the central suggestion of the 2026-09-25 revision plan without treating that plan as an instruction or an approved protocol.

## Research question

Within a participant, do targets rated as more part of, or inseparable from, self have word-association response profiles closer to that participant's self-reference profile on a different day? The first test concerns **target-specific self-inclusion**. Momentary boundary thinning, relationship closeness, positive feeling and prosocial behavior are distinct variables.

The directional candidate prediction is a negative within-person association between an independent self-inclusion rating and the distance from each non-self target to self. A null, reversed, unreliable or no-better-than-baseline relation counts against the proposed measurement approach. A relation does not establish that the profile reads an unconscious “true boundary” or that a new ontological entity has been measured.

## Candidate task and outcomes

| Item | Initial proposal; to be fixed before confirmatory collection |
| --- | --- |
| Targets | Self, four people chosen by a rule fixed in advance, and two non-human targets such as an animal and a natural entity. Record category and familiarity; protect third-party identities. Seven targets are an initial feasibility option, not a required population model. |
| Stimuli | Approximately 16 shared words for every target; choose and test them before confirmatory collection. A “self” word within an otherwise unreferenced list is not a self-reference condition. |
| Instruction | Briefly attend to the named target, then give one's own association to each word. Do not ask participants to become the target. Match timing, wording and interface across conditions. |
| Repetition | Two days. Seven targets × 16 words = 112 planned trials per day, or 224 per participant, before losses. Counterbalance target and word order and record fatigue and order effects. |
| Independent ratings | Ask directly about self-inclusion for each target, separately from response features. Also measure closeness/liking/importance as comparators. Test whether respondents distinguish “part of self” from “boundary feels thin”; IOS/IOS11 alone is not ground truth for the latter. |
| Primary profile | A preregistered word-by-feature response vector for each participant, day and target. Begin with a latency-only baseline; any skin-potential and semantic additions must show incremental value. Fix missingness handling, scaling and feature weights before looking at confirmatory relations. |
| Primary outcome | The prespecified within-participant association of target-specific rating with distance from each **non-self** target's profile to the self profile. Self-to-self distance is trivially zero and excluded. Specify whether the held-out day or average of days defines this estimate. |
| Reliability | Separately estimate signal repeatability, target-distance/rank stability, and repeatability of the rating–distance relationship. The old pooled latency `r=0.24` estimates none of these in the proposed study. |
| Alternatives | Test category, familiarity/closeness and lexical/response-time baselines; compare latency-only, latency profile, added physiology, added semantic content and a no-map profile-distance model. A display map is not itself a psychological measurement. |

## Analysis safeguards

Participants, not trials, are the independent sampling units. Multiple target distances from one person share the self baseline and must be modelled as dependent observations. Prespecify target eligibility, nonresponse, artefacts, missing features, outliers, transformations, distance metric, repeated-day rule, exclusions, covariates, uncertainty intervals and the main decision rule. Fit normalization and any tuning inside training partitions; assess generalization at participant level. Report individual variation as well as the pooled estimate. Do not select a visually appealing map and then treat its distances as a confirmatory endpoint.

The first roughly 10-person run is for comprehension, burden, usable signal and variance estimates. It is **not** a validation sample and yields no confirmed effect. Determine a later sample size with simulations or precision targets that allow for clustering, reliability and attrition. Freeze the confirmatory analysis and timestamp the protocol before examining confirmatory outcomes; an author proposal is not preregistration.

## Record and ethics requirements

Retain exact stimulus and instruction versions, target definitions, assignment/order, event timestamps, voice-detection method, device and software versions, raw signal units and sampling frequency, electrode locations, calibration, sensor failures, artefact flags and all exclusion reasons. Check whether the existing ethics approval and consent cover new self/other conditions, ratings, audio and physiological recordings; seek amendment or new approval as appropriate **before collection**. Keep real target names apart from analysis IDs. Define publication-safe derived data and access controls for audio, third-party names and sensitive associations.

## Manuscript path and open author decisions

The eventual empirical report should use Introduction → Methods → Results → Discussion → Limitations and state its actual data and preregistration status. No Results section should be drafted in advance. The five-person pilot can be cited as feasibility work, with its distinct task, sample and analysis clearly identified.

Decisions for Kawasaki, Tainaka and Takeuchi: which wording is the primary construct; whether equivalent self/target conditions already exist; target-selection rule; which ratings and Japanese versions may be used; whether day-two testing is feasible; exact sensor provenance; and whether to pursue this experiment before a separate theory paper. None is recorded here as approved.
