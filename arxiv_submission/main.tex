\begin{abstract}
Philosophical accounts often invoke ``spirit'' as a non-material essence, yet have lacked an operational definition compatible with physics. Here we introduce Spirit as a thermodynamic information quantity defined on a high-dimensional tensor field that couples linguistic, behavioural, physiological, affective, and temporal signals. This paper presents a theoretical framework based on three core assumptions: (1) Information is thermodynamic, (2) Spirituality is information, and (3) Spirit operates as an open thermodynamic system. We reinterpret the Rubber Hand Illusion as a reference phenomenon for information-induced self-boundary update, and propose a scalable Web-based Jungian word association protocol as a proxy for estimating the boundary at which external information becomes physiologically active for a person. We provide a tensor-first model, a geometry/topology pipeline, and an experimental methodology for measurement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The concept of ``spirit'' has long been central to philosophical and psychological inquiry, yet it has remained largely outside the scope of empirical science due to the absence of a physically grounded, measurable definition. This conceptual gap has hindered our ability to investigate the material basis of what are often considered non-material phenomena. To bridge this gap, we propose a fundamental re-conceptualization of Spirit, moving it from the metaphysical realm to the physical.

In this paper, we keep the Japanese term \jocho{} (情緒) untranslated as a compact label for a layer of lived, meaning-laden experience that is not fully captured by standard English categories such as ``emotion'' or ``affect,'' following Kiyoshi Oka's usage \cite{Oka1963,Oka2008}.

\section{Theoretical Framework}

To operationalize Spirit within the realm of physics, we establish three fundamental assumptions, creating a bridge from classical information theory to the thermodynamics of the psyche.

\subsection{Assumption 1: Information is Information Thermodynamics}
We posit that information is a physical quantity governed by thermodynamic laws.
First, \textbf{Shannon (1948)} mathematically defined information entropy ($H$), quantifying uncertainty \cite{Shannon1948}.
Subsequently, \textbf{Jaynes (1957)} demonstrated that this information entropy is formally equivalent to thermodynamic entropy ($S$) in statistical mechanics, thereby bridging information theory and physics \cite{Jaynes1957}.
Building on this, \textbf{Landauer (1961)} formulated the principle that the erasure of information incurs an energetic cost ($k_B T \ln 2$), proving that information processing is inextricably linked to heat generation \cite{Landauer1961}. This principle has been experimentally verified \cite{Berut2012}.
Thus, ``Spirit'' as an informational entity must obey these thermodynamic constraints.

\subsection{Assumption 2: Spirituality is Information}
We define ``Spirituality'' or the ``Self'' as a high-dimensional informational structure rather than a non-material essence.
The \textbf{Rubber Hand Illusion} \cite{Botvinick1998} demonstrates that the self-boundary is a malleable informational construct, not a fixed physical limit.
Similarly, \textbf{Jung's Word Association} experiments \cite{Jung1910} reveal that the psyche is structured into ``complexes''---clusters of information with measurable reaction times and emotional charges.
These findings imply that spiritual states can be mapped as structured information. In the first version of this model, the mapping was described as a vector space. In the present formulation, the vector representation is treated as a projection of a higher-order tensor field.

\subsection{Assumption 3: Spirit as Open System Information Thermodynamics}
The Spirit is modeled as an open thermodynamic system that constantly exchanges information and entropy with its environment \cite{Schrodinger1944}.
According to the \textbf{Free Energy Principle} proposed by \textbf{Friston (2010)}, biological agents minimize their free energy, which is equivalent to minimizing ``surprise'' (informational entropy) to maintain their structural integrity against the Second Law of Thermodynamics \cite{Friston2010}.
We formalize this as a state $S$ in an informational tensor field, where the energy potential $E$ corresponds to surprisal:
\begin{equation}
    E(w_I,w_O) = -\ln P(w_O | w_I, B, C) \quad (\text{Surprisal})
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    S = \{X, E, T, \mathcal{G}\}
\end{equation}
where $B$ denotes body-state observations, $C$ denotes session and environmental context, $X$ is the observed tensor field, and $\mathcal{G}$ is the induced geometry/topology of the response manifold.
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dS}{dt} = \frac{dS_{\text{internal}}}{dt} + \frac{dS_{\text{exchange}}}{dt}
\end{equation}
Here, the Spirit maintains its order (negative entropy) by processing information inputs ($w_I$) and generating outputs ($w_O$) that minimize the divergence between its internal model and external reality. Recent developments in non-equilibrium thermodynamics \cite{Seifert2012,Parrondo2015} and feedback control \cite{SagawaUeda2010} further support this formulation.

\subsection{Tensor Field Representation}
The canonical observation is a multi-way tensor:
\begin{equation}
    X[p,s,w,m,f,t],
\end{equation}
where $p$ indexes participant, $s$ indexes session or context, $w$ indexes the stimulus-response word pair, $m$ indexes measurement modality, $f$ indexes feature, and $t$ indexes time window. Modalities include language, reaction time, voice, facial expression, skin potential, heart-rate variability, pupil response, and motion when available. Features include semantic score, latency, amplitude, emotion score, arousal, uncertainty, entropy, and other normalized descriptors.

The participant-specific Spirit state is therefore:
\begin{equation}
    S_p(t) = X[p,:,:,:,:,t].
\end{equation}
The previous word vector representation is retained as a projection:
\begin{equation}
    \vec{v}_w = \Pi\left(X[p,:,w,:,:,:]\right),
\end{equation}
where $\Pi$ denotes a projection from the tensor field to a word-level embedding used for visualization or downstream graph construction.

\subsection{Tensor Contraction Probability Model}
The original association model used the inner product of word vectors, inverse reaction time, skin potential, and emotional expression. The tensor-first model generalizes this by defining association probability through contraction over semantic, physiological, affective, and contextual modes:
\begin{equation}
 P(w_O | w_I, B, C) =
 \frac{\exp\left(\mathcal{C}(X_{\text{lang}},X_{\text{body}},X_{\text{affect}},X_{\text{context}})\right)}{Z},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{C}(\cdot)$ is a learned or estimated tensor contraction and $Z$ is the normalizing partition function. The vector model is recovered when $\mathcal{C}$ is restricted to a semantic inner product plus scalar physiological factors.

\section{Structural Interpretation}
While the physical framework allows us to define and measure the Spirit manifold, understanding its internal structure requires an interpretative model. We use the concepts of analytical psychology as a model to interpret the physical structures we measure.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Complex:} An energy-charged node in the informational space.
    \item \textbf{Archetype:} A fundamental, structural template residing in the Collective Unconscious.
    \item \textbf{Shadow:} The unconscious and often suppressed part of a Complex.
\end{itemize}

\section{Measurement Methodology}
We use a modified version of the Word Association Experiment to probe the structure of this tensor field. The Rubber Hand Illusion is treated as a reference experiment: it demonstrates that external information can update the self-boundary when multisensory evidence is coherent. Because large-scale Rubber Hand Illusion experiments are costly, the Web-based word association protocol is used as a scalable proxy for estimating where external information begins to trigger coordinated physiological response.

The probability of association $P(w_O | w_I,B,C)$ is modeled to include semantic, behavioural, physiological, affective, and contextual components:

\begin{equation}
 P(w_O | w_I,B,C) =
 \frac{
 \exp(\mathcal{C}(X_{\text{lang}},X_{\text{body}},X_{\text{affect}},X_{\text{context}}))
 \cdot [r(w_I, w_O)]^{\alpha}
 \cdot \exp(\gamma \frac{\Delta SP}{\lambda})
 \cdot \exp(\eta F)
 }{Z}
\end{equation}

where $r$ is the inverse reaction time, $\Delta SP$ is skin potential arousal, and $F$ is emotional expression.

\subsection{Computational Pipeline}
The computational method consists of five complementary analyses.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Tensor decomposition.} CP, Tucker, or Tensor Train decomposition \cite{KoldaBader2009,Oseledets2011} extracts latent Spirit factors that couple word groups, modalities, affective states, and temporal dynamics.
    \item \textbf{Information geometry.} Participant responses are treated as conditional probability distributions, and Fisher information or related metrics \cite{Amari2016} estimate the local curvature of the response manifold.
    \item \textbf{Persistent homology.} Stable connected components, loops, and voids \cite{Edelsbrunner2002} are computed from the tensor-derived metric space. These topological invariants formalize clusters, repeated circuits, and missing or avoided regions.
    \item \textbf{Active inference and free energy.} Reaction delay, arousal, and expression change are interpreted as prediction error under the Free Energy Principle \cite{Friston2010}.
    \item \textbf{Adaptive intervention.} Later stimuli can be selected by expected information gain, allowing the experiment to probe boundary regions more efficiently than a fixed stimulus list.
\end{enumerate}

The 3D visualization is not the primary scientific object. It is a projection of the tensor topology into a human-readable space, where nodes, edges, loops, and voids help researchers inspect latent structure.

\section{Results}
We observed clear differentiation between stable archetypal structures (Spirit Types) and unstable shadow-driven interferences (Ghost Patterns).
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Spirit Types:} Integrated structures (Hero, Sage, Lover, Caregiver).
    \item \textbf{Ghost Patterns:} Unintegrated interferences (Individual Shadow, Collective Meme).
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
This paper introduces a paradigm shift, moving ``spirit'' from metaphysical abstraction to a concrete, physically measurable quantity. By operationalizing Spirit as a thermodynamic information quantity on a high-dimensional tensor field, and by treating 3D manifold visualization as a projection of tensor geometry and topology, we provide a framework that is theoretically coherent, computationally extensible, and experimentally scalable.
